## 1. Acids, Bases, and Reactive Intermediates

1. {What determines the acidity of an organic compound?} : {Acidity increases when the conjugate base is stabilized by electronegativity, resonance, induction, orbital hybridization, or solvation.}
    
2. {How does hybridization affect the acidity of a carbon–hydrogen bond?} : {Acidity increases in the order sp3 < sp2 < sp because orbitals with greater s character stabilize negative charge more effectively.}
    
3. {What is a nucleophile?} : {A nucleophile is an electron-rich species that donates an electron pair to form a new covalent bond.}
    
4. {What is an electrophile?} : {An electrophile is an electron-poor species that accepts an electron pair from a nucleophile.}
    
5. {What is a carbocation?} : {A carbocation is a positively charged, sp2-hybridized carbon intermediate whose stability generally follows methyl < primary < secondary < tertiary.}
    
6. {How does resonance affect carbocation stability?} : {Resonance stabilizes [^1]allylic and benzylic carbocations by distributing the positive charge over multiple atoms.}
    
7. {What is a carbanion?} : {A carbanion is a negatively charged carbon species whose stability increases through resonance, electron-withdrawing groups, and greater s character.}
    
8. {What is a radical?} : {A radical is a species containing an unpaired electron that commonly participates in chain reactions.}
    

## 2. Alkanes and Radical Reactions

9. {What occurs during radical halogenation of an alkane?} : {An alkane reacts with chlorine or bromine under heat or light through initiation, propagation, and termination steps to form an alkyl halide.}
    
10. {What happens in the initiation step of radical halogenation?} : {Heat or light causes homolytic cleavage of the halogen–halogen bond to produce two halogen radicals.}
    
11. {What happens in the propagation steps of radical halogenation?} : {A halogen radical removes hydrogen from the alkane, and the resulting carbon radical reacts with another halogen molecule.}
    
12. {Why is radical bromination more selective than radical chlorination?} : {Bromination has a more endothermic hydrogen-abstraction step and therefore favors formation of the most stable radical more strongly.}
    
13. {What does NBS accomplish in an alkene reaction?} : {NBS produces selective allylic bromination through a resonance-stabilized allylic radical mechanism.}
    
14. {What is radical autoxidation?} : {Radical autoxidation is a chain reaction in which carbon radicals react with oxygen to form peroxy radicals and hydroperoxides.}
    

## 3. Alkyl Halides: SN2 Reactions

15. {What is an SN2 reaction?} : {An SN2 reaction is a concerted substitution in which a nucleophile attacks from the backside while the leaving group departs.}
    
16. {What rate law describes an SN2 reaction?} : {The SN2 rate is proportional to both the substrate concentration and the nucleophile concentration.}
    
17. {What stereochemical result is produced by an SN2 reaction?} : {An SN2 reaction causes inversion of configuration at the reacting stereocenter.}
    
18. {Which substrates react fastest by the SN2 mechanism?} : {SN2 reactivity generally follows methyl > primary > secondary, while tertiary substrates are effectively unreactive because of steric hindrance.}
    
19. {Which solvents favor SN2 reactions?} : {Polar aprotic solvents favor SN2 reactions by solvating cations without strongly solvating anionic nucleophiles.}
    
20. {What makes a good leaving group in a substitution reaction?} : {A good leaving group is a relatively stable weak base such as iodide, bromide, chloride, tosylate, or water.}
    

## 4. Alkyl Halides: SN1 Reactions

21. {What is an SN1 reaction?} : {An SN1 reaction is a stepwise substitution involving leaving-group departure, carbocation formation, and nucleophilic attack.}
    
22. {What rate law describes an SN1 reaction?} : {The SN1 rate depends only on the concentration of the alkyl substrate.}
    
23. {Which substrates react fastest by the SN1 mechanism?} : {SN1 reactions favor tertiary, benzylic, and allylic substrates because they form relatively stable carbocations.}
    
24. {Which solvents favor SN1 reactions?} : {Polar protic solvents favor SN1 reactions by stabilizing the carbocation and leaving group through solvation.}
    
25. {What stereochemical result is expected from an SN1 reaction?} : {Attack on the planar carbocation can occur from either face and commonly produces partial or complete racemization.}
    
26. {Why can rearrangements occur during an SN1 reaction?} : {The carbocation intermediate can undergo hydride or alkyl shifts to produce a more stable carbocation before nucleophilic attack.}
    

## 5. Elimination Reactions

27. {What is an E2 reaction?} : {An E2 reaction is a concerted elimination in which a base removes a beta hydrogen as the leaving group departs and a double bond forms.}
    
28. {What geometry is required for an E2 reaction?} : {The beta hydrogen and leaving group normally must have an anti-periplanar arrangement.}
    
29. {What rate law describes an E2 reaction?} : {The E2 rate is proportional to both the substrate concentration and the base concentration.}
    
30. {What is Zaitsev’s rule?} : {Zaitsev’s rule states that elimination with a small base usually produces the more substituted alkene as the major product.}
    
31. {When is the Hofmann alkene favored?} : {A bulky base commonly removes the least hindered beta hydrogen and favors the less substituted Hofmann alkene.}
    
32. {What is an E1 reaction?} : {An E1 reaction is a stepwise elimination involving carbocation formation followed by removal of a beta proton.}
    
33. {Why do E1 and SN1 reactions often compete?} : {Both mechanisms proceed through the same carbocation intermediate under similar polar-protic and weak-nucleophile conditions.}
    
34. {How does heat affect substitution and elimination competition?} : {Higher temperature generally favors elimination because alkene formation produces more particles and has a favorable entropy change.}
    
35. {What conformational requirement applies to E2 elimination in cyclohexanes?} : {The leaving group and beta hydrogen must occupy trans-diaxial positions.}
    

## 6. Alkene Preparation

36. {How are alkenes prepared by alcohol dehydration?} : {Heating an alcohol with a strong acid removes water through an E1 or E2 mechanism to form an alkene.}
    
37. {How are alkenes prepared from alkyl halides?} : {Treatment of an alkyl halide with a strong base causes dehydrohalogenation by an E2 mechanism.}
    
38. {What does partial reduction of an alkyne with Lindlar’s catalyst produce?} : {Hydrogenation with Lindlar’s catalyst adds hydrogen syn and produces a cis alkene.}
    
39. {What does reduction of an alkyne with sodium in liquid ammonia produce?} : {Dissolving-metal reduction adds hydrogen anti and produces a trans alkene.}
    

## 7. Electrophilic Addition to Alkenes

40. {What is the product of alkene hydrohalogenation with HX?} : {HX adds across the double bond to form an alkyl halide, usually with Markovnikov regiochemistry.}
    
41. {Why does ordinary hydrohalogenation follow Markovnikov’s rule?} : {Protonation normally forms the more stable carbocation, which places the halide on the more substituted carbon.}
    
42. {What happens when HBr reacts with an alkene in the presence of peroxides?} : {HBr adds through a radical chain mechanism to give the anti-Markovnikov alkyl bromide.}
    
43. {Why is the peroxide effect generally limited to HBr?} : {Only the propagation steps involving HBr have favorable energetics for a sustained radical chain reaction.}
    
44. {What is the product of acid-catalyzed alkene hydration?} : {Water adds with Markovnikov regiochemistry through a carbocation intermediate to form an alcohol.}
    
45. {What are the disadvantages of acid-catalyzed hydration?} : {Carbocation rearrangements and polymerization can occur under the strongly acidic conditions.}
    
46. {What does oxymercuration–demercuration accomplish?} : {Mercuric acetate, water, and sodium borohydride convert an alkene into a Markovnikov alcohol without carbocation rearrangement.}
    
47. {What does hydroboration–oxidation of an alkene accomplish?} : {Borane followed by hydrogen peroxide and hydroxide forms an anti-Markovnikov alcohol through overall syn addition.}
    
48. {Why does hydroboration place boron on the less substituted carbon?} : {Steric and electronic effects favor attack that places boron on the less hindered carbon and developing positive charge on the more substituted carbon.}
    
49. {What occurs when an alkene reacts with bromine or chlorine?} : {A halogen adds anti across the double bond through a bridged halonium-ion intermediate.}
    
50. {What is formed when an alkene reacts with a halogen in water?} : {A halohydrin forms by anti addition, with hydroxyl generally appearing on the more substituted carbon.}
    
51. {What does catalytic hydrogenation of an alkene produce?} : {Hydrogen adds syn across the double bond over a metal catalyst to form an alkane.}
    
52. {What does epoxidation of an alkene with a peroxyacid produce?} : {A peroxyacid such as mCPBA converts an alkene stereospecifically into an epoxide.}
    
53. {What does syn dihydroxylation of an alkene produce?} : {Osmium tetroxide or cold dilute potassium permanganate adds two hydroxyl groups to the same face of an alkene.}
    
54. {How can an alkene be converted into an anti vicinal diol?} : {Epoxidation followed by acid- or base-catalyzed ring opening produces an anti vicinal diol.}
    
55. {What does ozonolysis do to an alkene?} : {Ozone cleaves the carbon–carbon double bond and converts its carbons into aldehydes, ketones, or carboxylic acids depending on the workup.}
    
56. {What does hot concentrated potassium permanganate do to an alkene?} : {It oxidatively cleaves the double bond to form ketones, carboxylic acids, or carbon dioxide.}
    
57. {What is alkene cyclopropanation?} : {A carbene or carbenoid adds across an alkene in a concerted stereospecific reaction to form a cyclopropane.}
    
58. {What does the Simmons–Smith reaction accomplish?} : {Diiodomethane and a zinc–copper reagent convert an alkene into a cyclopropane through syn addition.}
    

## 8. Alkyne Reactions

59. {How is a terminal alkyne converted into an acetylide ion?} : {A strong base such as sodium amide removes the terminal proton to form a carbon nucleophile.}
    
60. {How does an acetylide ion form a new carbon–carbon bond?} : {An acetylide ion attacks a methyl or primary alkyl halide through an SN2 reaction.}
    
61. {What happens when excess HX reacts with an alkyne?} : {Two Markovnikov additions usually produce a geminal dihalide.}
    
62. {What happens when one equivalent of halogen reacts with an alkyne?} : {Anti addition predominantly forms a trans dihaloalkene.}
    
63. {What does mercury-catalyzed hydration of a terminal alkyne produce?} : {Water adds in a Markovnikov manner to form an enol that tautomerizes into a methyl ketone.}
    
64. {What does hydroboration–oxidation of a terminal alkyne produce?} : {Anti-Markovnikov hydration forms an enol that tautomerizes into an aldehyde.}
    
65. {What does hydroboration–oxidation of an internal alkyne produce?} : {The reaction forms one or more ketones after enol–keto tautomerization.}
    
66. {What does exhaustive catalytic hydrogenation of an alkyne produce?} : {Two equivalents of hydrogen reduce the alkyne completely to an alkane.}
    
67. {What does oxidative cleavage of an alkyne produce?} : {Strong oxidation cleaves the triple bond to form carboxylic acids, with a terminal carbon becoming carbon dioxide.}
    

## 9. Conjugated Dienes and Pericyclic Reactions

68. {What products result from electrophilic addition to a conjugated diene?} : {Electrophilic addition can produce both the 1,2-addition product and the resonance-derived 1,4-addition product.}
    
69. {Which conjugated-diene product is kinetically favored?} : {The 1,2-addition product is often favored at lower temperature because it forms more rapidly.}
    
70. {Which conjugated-diene product is thermodynamically favored?} : {The 1,4-addition product is often favored at higher temperature because it usually contains the more stable alkene.}
    
71. {What is the Diels–Alder reaction?} : {A conjugated diene and a dienophile undergo a concerted 4+2 cycloaddition to form a six-membered ring.}
    
72. {What conformation must a diene adopt for a Diels–Alder reaction?} : {The diene must adopt an s-cis conformation so its terminal orbitals can overlap with the dienophile.}
    
73. {What is the endo rule in the Diels–Alder reaction?} : {When possible, the kinetically favored product places electron-withdrawing substituents beneath the developing ring system.}
    
74. {Why is the Diels–Alder reaction stereospecific?} : {Its concerted mechanism preserves the relative stereochemistry of substituents on the diene and dienophile.}
    

## 10. Aromaticity and Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution

75. {What makes a compound aromatic?} : {An aromatic compound must be cyclic, planar, fully conjugated, and contain 4n+2 pi electrons.}
    
76. {What makes a compound antiaromatic?} : {An antiaromatic compound is cyclic, planar, fully conjugated, and contains 4n pi electrons.}
    
77. {What is electrophilic aromatic substitution?} : {An electrophile replaces an aromatic hydrogen through electrophilic attack, formation of a resonance-stabilized sigma complex, and restoration of aromaticity.}
    
78. {How is benzene brominated?} : {Bromine and iron(III) bromide generate an electrophile that replaces a benzene hydrogen with bromine.}
    
79. {How is benzene chlorinated?} : {Chlorine and iron(III) chloride perform electrophilic aromatic substitution to produce chlorobenzene.}
    
80. {How is benzene nitrated?} : {Nitric acid and sulfuric acid generate the nitronium ion, which substitutes a benzene hydrogen to form nitrobenzene.}
    
81. {How is benzene sulfonated?} : {Sulfur trioxide in sulfuric acid reversibly installs a sulfonic acid group on the aromatic ring.}
    
82. {What occurs in Friedel–Crafts alkylation?} : {An alkyl halide and a Lewis acid add an alkyl group to an aromatic ring through electrophilic aromatic substitution.}
    
83. {What limitations affect Friedel–Crafts alkylation?} : {The reaction can undergo carbocation rearrangement and polyalkylation and generally fails on strongly deactivated rings.}
    
84. {What occurs in Friedel–Crafts acylation?} : {An acyl chloride or anhydride and a Lewis acid add an acyl group to an aromatic ring without rearrangement.}
    
85. {Why does Friedel–Crafts acylation usually stop after one substitution?} : {The newly installed carbonyl group deactivates the aromatic ring toward further electrophilic substitution.}
    
86. {Which substituents direct electrophilic aromatic substitution to the ortho and para positions?} : {Electron-donating groups and halogens generally direct incoming electrophiles to the ortho and para positions.}
    
87. {Which substituents direct electrophilic aromatic substitution to the meta position?} : {Most strongly electron-withdrawing groups direct incoming electrophiles to the meta position.}
    
88. {Why are halogens deactivating but ortho–para directing?} : {Halogens withdraw electron density inductively but donate electron density by resonance to stabilize ortho and para sigma complexes.}
    
89. {What does benzylic oxidation accomplish?} : {Strong oxidants convert an alkylbenzene containing at least one benzylic hydrogen into a benzoic acid derivative.}
    
90. {What occurs during benzylic halogenation?} : {NBS and light replace a benzylic hydrogen with bromine through a radical mechanism.}
    
91. {How can an aromatic nitro group be converted into an amino group?} : {Catalytic hydrogenation or metals in acid reduce the nitro group to an amine.}
    
92. {What is nucleophilic aromatic substitution by addition–elimination?} : {A nucleophile replaces an aromatic leaving group when electron-withdrawing groups stabilize the intermediate anionic sigma complex.}
    
93. {What is the benzyne mechanism?} : {A very strong base eliminates hydrogen halide from an aryl halide to form benzyne, which then undergoes nucleophilic addition and protonation.}
    

## 11. Alcohol Reactions

94. {How is an alkene converted into an alcohol?} : {Acid-catalyzed hydration, oxymercuration–demercuration, or hydroboration–oxidation adds the elements of water across the double bond.}
    
95. {How does sodium borohydride react with aldehydes and ketones?} : {Sodium borohydride reduces aldehydes to primary alcohols and ketones to secondary alcohols.}
    
96. {How does lithium aluminum hydride react with carbonyl compounds?} : {Lithium aluminum hydride strongly reduces aldehydes, ketones, carboxylic acids, esters, and many other carbonyl derivatives to alcohols.}
    
97. {What does PCC do to a primary alcohol?} : {PCC oxidizes a primary alcohol to an aldehyde without normally continuing to a carboxylic acid.}
    
98. {What does PCC do to a secondary alcohol?} : {PCC oxidizes a secondary alcohol to a ketone.}
    
99. {What do Jones reagent or dichromate do to a primary alcohol?} : {Strong aqueous chromium oxidants convert a primary alcohol into a carboxylic acid.}
    
100. {What does oxidation do to a tertiary alcohol?} : {A tertiary alcohol generally resists ordinary oxidation because its hydroxyl-bearing carbon has no hydrogen.}
    
101. {How is an alcohol converted into an alkyl chloride?} : {Thionyl chloride or phosphorus chlorides replace the hydroxyl group with chlorine.}
    
102. {How is an alcohol converted into an alkyl bromide?} : {Phosphorus tribromide commonly replaces the hydroxyl group with bromine through an SN2 pathway.}
    
103. {What is a tosylation reaction?} : {Tosyl chloride and a base convert an alcohol into a tosylate while retaining configuration at the carbon atom.}
    
104. {How are symmetrical ethers prepared by alcohol condensation?} : {Two primary alcohol molecules undergo acid-catalyzed dehydration to form an ether under controlled conditions.}
    

## 12. Ether and Epoxide Reactions

105. {What is the Williamson ether synthesis?} : {An alkoxide reacts with a methyl or primary alkyl halide through an SN2 mechanism to form an ether.}
    
106. {How are ethers cleaved by HI or HBr?} : {Protonation of the ether oxygen is followed by substitution to form an alkyl halide and an alcohol or a second alkyl halide.}
    
107. {How does a nucleophile open an epoxide under basic conditions?} : {The nucleophile attacks the less substituted epoxide carbon by an SN2 mechanism with inversion at the attacked carbon.}
    
108. {How does a nucleophile open an epoxide under acidic conditions?} : {After protonation, the nucleophile generally attacks the more substituted carbon with backside displacement and anti opening.}
    
109. {How does a Grignard reagent react with ethylene oxide?} : {It opens the epoxide and, after protonation, produces a primary alcohol with two additional carbon atoms.}
    

## 13. Organometallic Reactions

110. {How is a Grignard reagent prepared?} : {An alkyl, vinyl, or aryl halide reacts with magnesium in dry ether to form an organomagnesium halide.}
    
111. {Why must Grignard reactions be kept dry?} : {Water and other acidic substances protonate and destroy the strongly basic carbon nucleophile.}
    
112. {What does a Grignard reagent produce with formaldehyde?} : {Nucleophilic addition followed by protonation produces a primary alcohol.}
    
113. {What does a Grignard reagent produce with an aldehyde?} : {Nucleophilic addition followed by protonation usually produces a secondary alcohol.}
    
114. {What does a Grignard reagent produce with a ketone?} : {Nucleophilic addition followed by protonation produces a tertiary alcohol.}
    
115. {What does a Grignard reagent produce with carbon dioxide?} : {Addition to carbon dioxide followed by acidic workup produces a carboxylic acid containing one additional carbon.}
    
116. {How do organolithium reagents behave toward carbonyl compounds?} : {Organolithium reagents act as powerful carbon nucleophiles and add to carbonyl groups similarly to Grignard reagents.}
    
117. {What is the Gilman coupling reaction?} : {A lithium dialkylcuprate couples with many primary alkyl halides to form a new carbon–carbon bond.}
    

## 14. Aldehydes and Ketones

118. {What is the general mechanism of nucleophilic addition to a carbonyl group?} : {A nucleophile attacks the electrophilic carbonyl carbon, producing a tetrahedral alkoxide that is subsequently protonated.}
    
119. {Why are aldehydes generally more reactive than ketones?} : {Aldehydes have less steric hindrance and receive less electron donation from alkyl groups.}
    
120. {How does HCN react with an aldehyde or ketone?} : {Cyanide adds to the carbonyl carbon and protonation produces a cyanohydrin.}
    
121. {How does an alcohol react with an aldehyde or ketone under acidic conditions?} : {One alcohol equivalent forms a hemiacetal, while excess alcohol forms an acetal through acid-catalyzed substitution.}
    
122. {Why are acetals useful in organic synthesis?} : {Acetals protect aldehydes and ketones from bases and nucleophiles and can later be removed with aqueous acid.}
    
123. {How does a primary amine react with an aldehyde or ketone?} : {A primary amine undergoes condensation with the carbonyl compound to form an imine.}
    
124. {How does a secondary amine react with an aldehyde or ketone?} : {A secondary amine undergoes condensation to form an enamine.}
    
125. {What is the Wittig reaction?} : {A phosphorus ylide converts an aldehyde or ketone into an alkene while producing triphenylphosphine oxide.}
    
126. {What does the Clemmensen reduction accomplish?} : {Zinc amalgam and hydrochloric acid reduce an aldehyde or ketone carbonyl group to a methylene group under acidic conditions.}
    
127. {What does the Wolff–Kishner reduction accomplish?} : {Hydrazine and strong base reduce an aldehyde or ketone carbonyl group to a methylene group under basic conditions.}
    
128. {How does Tollens’ reagent distinguish aldehydes from most ketones?} : {Tollens’ reagent oxidizes an aldehyde to a carboxylate while producing a silver mirror.}
    
129. {What is the Baeyer–Villiger oxidation?} : {A peroxyacid inserts oxygen next to a ketone carbonyl to form an ester or converts a cyclic ketone into a lactone.}
    

## 15. Carboxylic Acids and Their Derivatives

130. {How is a carboxylic acid formed by oxidation?} : {A primary alcohol or aldehyde can be oxidized with a strong oxidant to produce a carboxylic acid.}
    
131. {How is a nitrile converted into a carboxylic acid?} : {Acidic or basic hydrolysis converts the nitrile carbon into the carboxyl carbon.}
    
132. {What is nucleophilic acyl substitution?} : {A nucleophile adds to an acyl carbon, forming a tetrahedral intermediate that eliminates a leaving group to restore the carbonyl.}
    
133. {What is the usual reactivity order of carboxylic acid derivatives?} : {Reactivity generally decreases in the order acyl halide > anhydride > ester approximately carboxylic acid > amide.}
    
134. {How is an acid chloride converted into an ester?} : {An alcohol attacks the acid chloride through nucleophilic acyl substitution, usually in the presence of a base.}
    
135. {How is an acid chloride converted into an amide?} : {Ammonia or an amine attacks the acid chloride and replaces chloride to form an amide.}
    
136. {How is an acid chloride reduced to an aldehyde?} : {A mild selective reducing agent such as lithium tri-tert-butoxyaluminum hydride can stop the reduction at the aldehyde.}
    
137. {What does a Grignard reagent do to an acid chloride or ester?} : {Two additions occur, and acidic workup normally produces a tertiary alcohol.}
    
138. {What is Fischer esterification?} : {A carboxylic acid and an alcohol reversibly form an ester and water under acid catalysis.}
    
139. {What is ester hydrolysis under acidic conditions?} : {An ester reacts reversibly with water under acid catalysis to produce a carboxylic acid and an alcohol.}
    
140. {What is saponification?} : {Hydroxide irreversibly hydrolyzes an ester to form a carboxylate salt and an alcohol.}
    
141. {What is transesterification?} : {An ester exchanges its alkoxy group with an alcohol under acid or base catalysis.}
    
142. {How is an ester reduced by lithium aluminum hydride?} : {The acyl portion becomes a primary alcohol and the alkoxy leaving group also becomes an alcohol.}
    
143. {How is an ester partially reduced to an aldehyde?} : {DIBAL-H at low temperature can reduce many esters to aldehydes after controlled workup.}
    
144. {How is an amide hydrolyzed?} : {Heating with aqueous acid or base converts an amide into a carboxylic acid or carboxylate and an ammonium or amine product.}
    
145. {What does lithium aluminum hydride produce from an amide?} : {It replaces the amide carbonyl oxygen with hydrogen to produce an amine.}
    
146. {What is the Hofmann rearrangement?} : {A primary amide reacts with bromine and base to form a primary amine containing one fewer carbon atom.}
    
147. {What is decarboxylation?} : {Decarboxylation removes carbon dioxide, especially readily from beta-keto acids and malonic acid derivatives.}
    

## 16. Enols, Enolates, and Alpha-Carbon Reactions

148. {What is keto–enol tautomerism?} : {A carbonyl compound and its enol interconvert through proton transfer and movement of the double bond.}
    
149. {Why are alpha hydrogens next to carbonyl groups acidic?} : {Their removal forms an enolate whose negative charge is stabilized by resonance with the carbonyl oxygen.}
    
150. {What is alpha halogenation of a carbonyl compound?} : {An enol or enolate attacks a halogen electrophile and installs halogen at the alpha carbon.}
    
151. {What is the haloform reaction?} : {A methyl ketone reacts with excess halogen and hydroxide to form a carboxylate and a haloform such as chloroform or iodoform.}
    
152. {What is kinetic enolate formation?} : {A strong bulky base at low temperature rapidly removes the less hindered alpha proton to produce the less substituted enolate.}
    
153. {What is thermodynamic enolate formation?} : {Reversible deprotonation under equilibrating conditions favors the more substituted and more stable enolate.}
    
154. {What is direct enolate alkylation?} : {An enolate attacks a methyl or primary alkyl halide through an SN2 reaction to form a new carbon–carbon bond.}
    
155. {What is the aldol addition?} : {An enolate adds to another aldehyde or ketone to form a beta-hydroxy carbonyl compound.}
    
156. {What is aldol condensation?} : {An aldol product loses water to form an alpha,beta-unsaturated carbonyl compound.}
    
157. {What is a crossed aldol reaction?} : {The enolate of one carbonyl compound reacts with a different aldehyde or ketone.}
    
158. {What is an intramolecular aldol reaction?} : {A molecule containing two carbonyl groups cyclizes through enolate addition and often dehydrates to form a five- or six-membered ring.}
    
159. {What is the Claisen condensation?} : {An ester enolate attacks another ester to form a beta-keto ester after elimination of an alkoxide.}
    
160. {What is the Dieckmann condensation?} : {An intramolecular Claisen condensation forms a cyclic beta-keto ester.}
    
161. {What is the malonic ester synthesis?} : {A malonate ester is deprotonated, alkylated, hydrolyzed, and decarboxylated to produce a substituted acetic acid.}
    
162. {What is the acetoacetic ester synthesis?} : {A beta-keto ester is deprotonated, alkylated, hydrolyzed, and decarboxylated to produce a substituted methyl ketone.}
    
163. {What is conjugate or 1,4-addition?} : {A nucleophile attacks the beta carbon of an alpha,beta-unsaturated carbonyl compound and generates an enolate that is subsequently protonated.}
    
164. {What is the Michael reaction?} : {A resonance-stabilized carbon nucleophile adds to an alpha,beta-unsaturated carbonyl compound through conjugate addition.}
    
165. {What is the Robinson annulation?} : {A Michael addition followed by intramolecular aldol condensation forms a substituted cyclohexenone ring.}
    

## 17. Amines

166. {How are amines commonly prepared from alkyl halides?} : {Ammonia or an amine performs nucleophilic substitution, although repeated alkylation can produce mixtures and quaternary ammonium salts.}
    
167. {What is the Gabriel synthesis?} : {Phthalimide is alkylated through an SN2 reaction and then cleaved to produce a primary amine.}
    
168. {How are nitriles reduced to amines?} : {Lithium aluminum hydride or catalytic hydrogenation converts a nitrile into a primary amine.}
    
169. {What is reductive amination?} : {An aldehyde or ketone reacts with ammonia or an amine to form an imine or iminium ion that is reduced to an amine.}
    
170. {How are amines acylated?} : {An amine attacks an acid chloride or anhydride through nucleophilic acyl substitution to form an amide.}
    
171. {What is exhaustive methylation followed by Hofmann elimination?} : {An amine is converted into a quaternary ammonium salt that undergoes E2 elimination to favor the least substituted alkene.}
    
172. {How is an aromatic diazonium salt prepared?} : {A primary aromatic amine reacts with nitrous acid at low temperature to form an arenediazonium ion.}
    
173. {What is the Sandmeyer reaction?} : {A diazonium group is replaced by chlorine, bromine, or cyanide using the corresponding copper(I) salt.}
    
174. {How can a diazonium group be replaced by hydroxyl?} : {Heating an arenediazonium salt with water produces a phenol and releases nitrogen gas.}
    
175. {What is azo coupling?} : {An arenediazonium ion acts as an electrophile toward an activated aromatic ring to form a colored azo compound.}
    

## 18. Phenols

176. {Why is phenol more acidic than an ordinary alcohol?} : {The phenoxide conjugate base is stabilized by resonance delocalization into the aromatic ring.}
    
177. {How does bromine react with phenol?} : {The strongly activated phenol ring undergoes rapid ortho and para bromination, often producing 2,4,6-tribromophenol with excess bromine.}
    
178. {What is the Williamson synthesis of an aryl ether?} : {A phenoxide ion attacks a methyl or primary alkyl halide through an SN2 reaction to form an alkoxybenzene.}
    
179. {What is the Claisen rearrangement of an allyl aryl ether?} : {Heating moves the allyl group from oxygen to an ortho ring carbon through a concerted sigmatropic rearrangement.}
    

## 19. Biological Organic Reactions

180. {How is a glycosidic bond formed?} : {The anomeric hydroxyl group of a carbohydrate reacts with an alcohol or another sugar to form an acetal linkage.}
    
181. {What is mutarotation?} : {Mutarotation is the interconversion of alpha and beta carbohydrate anomers through the open-chain form.}
    
182. {How is a peptide bond formed?} : {The amino group of one amino acid forms an amide bond with the carboxyl group of another amino acid.}
    
183. {What reaction hydrolyzes a peptide bond?} : {Acid, base, or a protease catalyzes nucleophilic acyl substitution that cleaves the amide linkage.}
    
184. {What reaction forms a triglyceride?} : {Glycerol undergoes esterification with three fatty acids to form a triester.}
    
185. {What happens during fat saponification?} : {Base hydrolysis of triglycerides produces glycerol and the carboxylate salts known as soap.}
    
186. {What is nucleotide phosphodiester-bond formation?} : {A phosphate group forms ester linkages between the 3-prime hydroxyl of one sugar and the 5-prime hydroxyl of another.}
    
187. {What is ATP hydrolysis?} : {Water cleaves a phosphoanhydride bond in ATP to produce lower-energy phosphate products and release usable free energy.}
