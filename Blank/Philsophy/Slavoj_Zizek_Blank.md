## Topic 1: Ideology and everyday life

### A. Ideology beyond conscious belief

1. {Why does Žižek discuss people who understand capitalism’s problems but continue participating in it?} : {Knowing that a system is problematic does not free us from ideology when our actions continue sustaining it.}
    
2. {Why does Žižek examine money as an example of commodity fetishism?} : {Our practical treatment of money can sustain beliefs that we consciously dismiss as illusions.}
    

Source: [The Sublime Object of Ideology, excerpt](https://english.hku.hk/staff/kjohnson/PDF/ZizekSUBLIMEOBJECT.pdf).

### B. Seeing ideology

3. {Why does Žižek discuss the sunglasses that reveal hidden commands in They Live?} : {The glasses expose the ideological demands concealed within apparently ordinary advertisements and social reality.}
    
4. {Why does Žižek emphasize the fight over putting on those sunglasses?} : {Leaving ideology can be painful because it disrupts the attachments through which we experience our world.}
    

Source: [The Pervert’s Guide to Ideology, opening sequence](https://zizek.org/talk/the-pervert-s-guide-to-ideology-2012).

## Topic 2: The big Other and symbolic authority

### A. Social reality and its guarantees

5. {Why does Žižek interpret the computer system in The Matrix as a version of the big Other?} : {It represents the symbolic network that organizes people’s reality and gives their actions social meaning.}
    
6. {Why does Žižek criticize The Matrix for imagining a controlling system behind everything?} : {The film gives concrete existence to an ultimate guarantor whose absence and inconsistency psychoanalysis asks us to confront.}
    

Source: [The Matrix, or, the Two Sides of Perversion](https://www.lacan.com/zizek-matrix.htm).

### B. Delegated belief and enjoyment

7. {What does Žižek’s example of a Tibetan prayer wheel suggest about belief?} : {Belief can operate through external rituals rather than requiring continuous inner conviction.}
    
8. {Why does Žižek discuss television’s canned laughter?} : {The soundtrack can perform enjoyment on our behalf even when we remain silent and tired.}
    

Source: [The Interpassive Subject](https://www.lacan.com/zizek-pompidou.htm).

## Topic 3: Desire, fantasy, and commodities

### A. The elusive object of desire

9. {Why does Žižek examine the plastic toy inside a Kinder Surprise egg?} : {The toy materializes the promised surplus that makes the commodity seem to contain something beyond its ordinary usefulness.}
    
10. {Why does Žižek compare Kinder eggs with purchases offering free extras?} : {The bonus promises to compensate for a satisfaction that the commodity itself never fully delivers.}
    

### B. Fantasy and supposedly harmless enjoyment

11. {Why does Žižek discuss decaffeinated coffee alongside other products stripped of unwanted properties?} : {These products illustrate a desire to enjoy something while avoiding the disturbing substance associated with it.}
    
12. {Why does Žižek connect tolerance with accepting an Other deprived of disturbing characteristics?} : {Such tolerance can welcome cultural difference only after removing what makes another person difficult to accommodate.}
    

Source: [Human Rights in a Chocolate Egg](https://www.cabinetmagazine.org/issues/11/zizek.php).

## Topic 4: Enjoyment, guilt, and the superego

### A. Permission as pressure

13. {Why does Žižek contrast an authoritarian father with a permissive father asking a child to visit grandmother?} : {The permissive father can demand both obedience and a freely felt desire to obey.}
    
14. {Why is the apparently optional grandmother visit more coercive in Žižek’s example?} : {It makes the child responsible for wanting what the parent has already required.}
    

Source: [Jacques Lacan: A Lateral Introduction, Part 3](https://www.zizek.org/talk/slavoj-zizek-masterclass-jacques-lacan-a-lateral-introduction-part-3?t=1855).

### B. The obligation to enjoy

15. {Why does Žižek describe a father ordering his children to enjoy an organized holiday?} : {Turning enjoyment into an obligation can produce guilt and inhibit the pleasure it demands.}
    
16. {Why does Žižek discuss recording films that he rarely watches?} : {The recording device provides delegated satisfaction, as though storing the films accomplishes their enjoyment for him.}
    

Source: [The Interpassive Subject](https://www.lacan.com/zizek-pompidou.htm).

## Topic 5: Hegel, negation, and the parallax view

### A. Determinate absence

17. {Why does Žižek repeat the joke about ordering coffee without cream and receiving coffee without milk?} : {Identical material objects can have different symbolic determinations because the particular absence matters.}
    
18. {Why does Žižek discuss an unplayed third musical line in Schumann’s Humoreske?} : {An absent element can organize how we experience what is materially present.}
    

Source: [“Is It Still Possible to Be a Hegelian Today?”](https://www.zizekstudies.org/index.php/IJZS/article/download/97/96).

### B. Irreducible perspectives

19. {Why does Žižek discuss two groups drawing different maps of the same Winnebago village?} : {Their incompatible maps register a social antagonism that no neutral picture alone can explain.}
    
20. {Why does Žižek compare the two arrangements of Fitzgerald’s Tender Is the Night?} : {The unresolved gap between the versions reveals a difficulty within the represented social reality itself.}
    

Source: [The Parallax View, excerpt](https://www.lacan.com/zizparallax.htm).

## Topic 6: Subjectivity and the unconscious

### A. The subject beyond a unified self

21. {Why does Žižek discuss Marcel hearing his grandmother’s voice over the telephone in Proust?} : {Separated from her visible body, the voice becomes an uncanny partial object with an unusually intimate presence.}
    
22. {Why does Žižek emphasize that this telephone voice feels both distant and excessively close?} : {The encounter shows how an object can disturb the ordinary boundary between external reality and intimate experience.}
    

Source: [The Grandmother’s Voice](https://www.lacan.com/lacinkXXXVIII9.html).

### B. Knowledge we do not acknowledge

23. {Why does Žižek add unknown knowns to Donald Rumsfeld’s categories of knowledge?} : {The added category names beliefs and assumptions we possess without consciously acknowledging them.}
    
24. {Why does Žižek compare these unknown knowns with the Freudian unconscious?} : {The unconscious includes effective knowledge that shapes us without presenting itself as knowledge we recognize.}
    

Source: [Censorship Today: Violence, or Ecology as a New Opium for the Masses, Part II](https://www.lacan.com/zizecology2.htm).

## Topic 7: Capitalism, charity, and ethical consumption

### A. Buying moral reassurance

25. {Why does Žižek analyze Starbucks advertising that links buying coffee with helping communities?} : {The purchase combines consumption with the feeling that one has already fulfilled an ethical responsibility.}
    
26. {Why does Žižek discuss TOMS’s former one-for-one shoe campaign?} : {The campaign exemplifies a commodity whose purchase incorporates a charitable justification for consuming it.}
    

### B. Cultural capitalism

27. {Why does Žižek discuss the reassurance associated with buying organic apples?} : {The purchase can offer an ethical self-image in addition to the product’s material qualities.}
    
28. {Why does Žižek contrast separate charitable donations with charity built into a purchase?} : {Cultural capitalism increasingly sells consumption and its moral compensation in the same transaction.}
    

Source: [First as Tragedy, Then as Farce, lecture transcript](https://www.zizek.org/talk/slavoj-zizek-first-as-tragedy-then-as-farce).

## Topic 8: Violence, language, and social explanation

### A. Visible and invisible violence

29. {Why does Žižek insist that apparently irrational violent outbursts be considered alongside systemic violence?} : {Visible acts occur against a background of less visible coercion that helps establish what counts as normal life.}
    
30. {Why does Žižek resist treating language simply as a peaceful alternative to violence?} : {Language can impose classifications and meanings that exclude or subordinate others.}
    

Source: [Lacan masterclass, Lecture 1](https://zizek.org/talk/slavoj-zizek-masterclass-on-lacan-may-june-06-lecture-1-restored-audio).

### B. Explanation and responsibility

31. {Why does Žižek describe a neo-Nazi explaining his violence through sociological accounts of his upbringing?} : {A sophisticated explanation of one’s behavior can coexist with continuing that behavior rather than transforming it.}
    
32. {Why does Žižek question replacing the category of worker with immigrant in political discussion?} : {The substitution can turn questions of exploitation into questions of cultural tolerance while obscuring class relations.}
    

Source: [Self-Deceptions](https://www.lacan.com/zizek-self.htm).

## Topic 9: Freedom, political action, and universality

### A. Choice within established limits

33. {Why does Žižek discuss the French television program C’est mon choix?} : {Its celebration of unusual lifestyles illustrates freedom exercised within an already established framework of acceptable choices.}
    
34. {Why does Žižek distinguish changing personal choices from changing the framework of choice?} : {Political freedom includes the capacity to alter the conditions that determine which choices are available.}
    

### B. Repeating a revolutionary gesture

35. {Why does Žižek argue for returning to Lenin without simply restoring Lenin’s historical program?} : {The task is to renew the possibility of a political break under different conditions rather than reproduce past institutions.}
    
36. {Why does Žižek criticize dismissing every challenge to liberal capitalism as a path to another gulag?} : {The dismissal can prohibit serious examination of alternatives before their actual arguments are considered.}
    

Source: [A Plea for Leninist Intolerance](https://www.lacan.com/zizek-plea.htm).

## Topic 10: Christianity, atheism, and collective emancipation

### A. Christ’s death and revolutionary community

37. {Why does Žižek challenge readings of Saint Paul that treat Christ’s death merely as preparation for resurrection?} : {He regards death and negativity as central to the transformation that makes a new community possible.}
    
38. {Why does Žižek discuss Paul’s community as a model for emancipatory collectivities?} : {It suggests a collective attachment capable of reorganizing established identities and social divisions.}
    

Source: [On Divine Self-Limitation and Revolutionary Love](https://www.lacan.com/zizpaul.htm).

### B. The Holy Spirit and human relations

39. {Why does Žižek interpret the Holy Spirit as departing from the sequence of father and son?} : {It names a community that exceeds the organization of collective life through paternal genealogy.}
    
40. {Why does Žižek locate the divine in encounters between people rather than withdrawal into hidden knowledge?} : {His reading makes transformative human relationships central to Christianity’s philosophical significance.}
    

Sources: [A Glance into the Archives of Islam](https://www.lacan.com/zizarchives.htm), [On Divine Self-Limitation and Revolutionary Love](https://www.lacan.com/zizpaul.htm).

## Topic 11: Ecology, catastrophe, and responsibility

### A. Knowledge without belief

41. {Why does Žižek discuss Chernobyl’s apparently ordinary landscape?} : {An environment can look familiar while containing dangers that ordinary perception cannot register.}
    
42. {Why does Žižek use the joke about a man who knows he is not grain but fears chickens?} : {Knowing a fact does not necessarily transform the externalized beliefs through which we act.}
    

### B. Ecology without an idealized Nature

43. {Why does Žižek reject the image of Nature as a harmonious order disrupted only by humanity?} : {That image obscures instability and offers a misleading guarantee that a balanced world awaits our retreat.}
    
44. {Why does Žižek argue that ecological responsibility requires unlearning familiar assumptions?} : {Trust in the ordinary continuity of our world can prevent us from taking catastrophic possibilities seriously.}
    

Source: [Censorship Today: Violence, or Ecology as a New Opium for the Masses, Part II](https://www.lacan.com/zizecology2.htm).
