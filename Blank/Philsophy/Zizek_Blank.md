## Topic 1: Ideology and belief

### A. Knowing versus doing

1. {What does Žižek’s example of people using money despite knowing it has no magical properties reveal about ideology?} : {Ideology operates through practices as well as conscious beliefs, because people can understand that money depends on social relations while exchanging it as though value were an inherent property of the object.} [Source: _The Sublime Object of Ideology_](https://xenopraxis.net/readings/zizek_sublimeobject.pdf)
    
2. {Why does knowing how money works fail to eliminate commodity fetishism in Žižek’s analysis?} : {Intellectual understanding does not automatically change the practices through which relations between people take the appearance of relations between commodities.} [Source](https://xenopraxis.net/readings/zizek_sublimeobject.pdf)
    
3. {How does Žižek’s example of coins retaining their monetary function despite physical wear explain the sublime object?} : {An ordinary object acquires a symbolic status exceeding its physical properties, so its socially supported identity appears to persist beyond the deterioration of its material body.} [Source](https://xenopraxis.net/readings/zizek_sublimeobject.pdf)
    

### B. Belief outside the individual

4. {What does the Tibetan prayer wheel illustrate in Žižek’s account of belief?} : {A ritual object can perform belief on someone’s behalf, showing that belief can reside in external practices rather than exclusively in an individual’s sincere inner conviction.} [Source: “The Interpassive Subject”](https://www.lacan.com/zizek-pompidou.htm)
    
5. {Why does Žižek regard conventional greetings as more than hypocrisy?} : {A greeting can establish a real symbolic bond even when neither speaker experiences the emotion its words literally express.} [Source](https://www.lacan.com/zizek-pompidou.htm)
    

## Topic 2: The subject and the symbolic order

### A. The big Other

6. {What does Žižek’s example of a judge performing an institutional act explain about the big Other?} : {The big Other is the symbolic network of rules and institutions that gives an utterance authority beyond the personal intentions or qualities of the individual speaking.} [Source](https://www.lacan.com/zizek-pompidou.htm)
    
7. {How does Žižek’s discussion of Freud’s murdered primordial father challenge the idea that removing authority produces unrestricted enjoyment?} : {The father’s death establishes prohibition through his surviving symbolic authority, showing that eliminating an external obstacle can strengthen the law rather than release desire from every limit.} [Source: “The Big Other Doesn’t Exist”](https://www.lacan.com/zizekother.htm)
    
8. {Why does Žižek connect fantasies of a leader monopolizing enjoyment with the decline of symbolic authority?} : {The despotic leader gives an identifiable human form to an impossibility within desire itself, allowing people to imagine that enjoyment would become complete if this supposed monopolist disappeared.} [Source](https://www.lacan.com/zizekother.htm)
    

### B. The divided subject

9. {What does Žižek’s example of someone experiencing genuine emotions through a fictional online persona reveal about subjectivity?} : {The subject is divided because feelings expressed through an apparently artificial identity can disclose desires that the person’s consciously accepted self-image excludes.} [Source: “The Interpassive Subject”](https://www.lacan.com/zizek-pompidou.htm)

## Topic 3: Desire, fantasy, and enjoyment

### A. Fantasy teaches desire

10. {Why does Žižek ask how someone comes to desire strawberry cake rather than simply imagining eating an unavailable cake?} : {Fantasy supplies the scenario that makes an object desirable in the first place, rather than merely compensating for the object’s absence.} [Source: _How to Read Lacan_](https://www.lacan.com/essays/)
    
11. {What does Žižek’s description of satisfaction from circling a desired object explain about drive?} : {Drive finds satisfaction in repeatedly pursuing and circling an obstacle, so its enjoyment can persist even when the pursuit fails to secure its declared goal.} [Source](https://www.lacan.com/essays/)
    
12. {How does Žižek’s example of a compulsively repeated tic explain the sinthome?} : {A sinthome is a particular organization of signs and enjoyment that sustains a subject’s way of existing and cannot be exhausted by interpreting it as a hidden message.} [Source](https://www.lacan.com/essays/)
    

### B. Delegated enjoyment

13. {What does television’s canned laughter illustrate in Žižek’s theory of interpassivity?} : {The soundtrack can perform the viewer’s enjoyment on their behalf, allowing them to feel relieved without personally laughing.} [Source: “The Interpassive Subject”](https://www.lacan.com/zizek-pompidou.htm)
    
14. {Why does Žižek describe satisfaction from recording films that he never watches?} : {The recorder symbolically watches for him, allowing possession and storage to substitute for the supposedly intended experience of viewing.} [Source: “The Interpassive Subject”](https://www.lacan.com/interpass.htm)
    
15. {How does Žižek’s example of advertisements displaying the consumer’s delighted reaction relate to the superego?} : {Advertising presents enjoyment as an expected response and can simultaneously perform that response for us, revealing how pleasure becomes a duty that people seek to delegate.} [Source](https://www.lacan.com/interpass.htm)
    

## Topic 4: Hegel, negation, and historical meaning

### A. Absence has a specific meaning

16. {What does Žižek’s joke about receiving coffee without milk instead of coffee without cream explain about determinate negation?} : {An absence has meaning through what specifically is absent, so materially identical coffees can occupy different symbolic positions depending on whether milk or cream is negated.} [Source: “Is God Dead, Unconscious, Evil, Impotent, Stupid … Or Just Counterfactual?”](https://zizekstudies.org/index.php/IJZS/article/download/97/96)
    
17. {Why does Žižek connect the coffee joke to the political route toward universality?} : {A universal position acquires its character through the particular exclusions it overcomes, so affirming universal equality requires examining whose supposedly universal standpoint already dominates.} [Source](https://zizekstudies.org/index.php/IJZS/article/download/97/96)
    

### B. Meaning emerges retroactively

18. {Why does Žižek recommend reading a Lacan seminar and then its corresponding written essay?} : {The later encounter with a precise formulation retroactively clarifies the earlier exploration, illustrating how understanding develops through a return that transforms the significance of what preceded it.} [Source: _How to Read Lacan_](https://www.lacan.com/essays/)
    
19. {How does Žižek’s example of a commodity becoming saleable value explain retroactivity?} : {Production generates value that must be socially realized through exchange, so the successful sale retrospectively confirms the product’s place within capitalist reproduction.} [Source: _The Parallax View_, excerpt III](https://www.lacan.com/zizparallax3.htm)
    

## Topic 5: Parallax, reality, and the Real

### A. Perspectives reveal antagonism

20. {What does Žižek learn from Lévi-Strauss’s example of village members drawing incompatible maps of the same settlement?} : {Their different maps express opposing attempts to organize an unresolved social antagonism, so the disagreement reveals a division within their shared social reality.} [Source: _The Parallax View_, excerpt I](https://www.lacan.com/zizparallax.htm)
    
21. {Why is the Real in Žižek’s village-map example different from the settlement’s objectively measurable layout?} : {The Real is the antagonism that prevents the community from representing itself as a harmonious whole, rather than simply the physical arrangement that a more accurate map could record.} [Source](https://www.lacan.com/zizparallax.htm)
    
22. {How does Žižek’s village-map example distinguish parallax from simple relativism?} : {The conflicting perspectives are connected by the same unresolved antagonism, making their incompatibility evidence of a shared structural problem rather than merely separate subjective preferences.} [Source](https://www.lacan.com/zizparallax.htm)
    

### B. Appearance can disclose truth

23. {Why does Žižek discuss aristocrats who make worse aristocratic models than ordinary people in Henry James’s “The Real Thing”?} : {Social identity and its convincing representation do not coincide, because supposedly authentic aristocratic being can fail to produce the appearance through which aristocracy becomes recognizable.} [Source](https://www.lacan.com/zizparallax.htm)

## Topic 6: Sexuality and social fantasy

### A. The absence of guaranteed harmony

24. {What does Žižek mean when he connects personal sexual fantasies to Lacan’s statement that there is no sexual relationship?} : {There is no universal symbolic formula guaranteeing complementary relations between partners, so desire depends on particular fantasies rather than a naturally assured harmony.} [Source: _The Parallax View_, excerpt II](https://www.lacan.com/zizparallax2.htm)

### B. Fantasy organizes political explanation

25. {Why does Žižek discuss the unemployed single mother as a supposed source of Britain’s social problems?} : {The figure gives a concrete imaginary body to diffuse anxieties, allowing structural problems to be explained through blame directed at an identifiable social group.} [Source](https://www.lacan.com/zizparallax2.htm)
    
26. {How does Žižek’s reading of the shark in _Jaws_ explain ideological condensation?} : {The shark gathers multiple fears into one threatening object, allowing different viewers to experience a common enemy without resolving the conflicts underlying their fears.} [Source](https://www.lacan.com/zizparallax2.htm)
    
27. {Why does Žižek describe Nazi antisemitism as organizing otherwise disconnected anxieties?} : {The antisemitic signifier falsely unifies economic, political, and cultural troubles into a conspiracy narrative that conceals social antagonisms by blaming Jews.} [Source](https://www.lacan.com/zizparallax2.htm)
    

## Topic 7: Capitalism, consumption, and charity

### A. Ethical consumption

28. {What does Žižek’s example of Starbucks selling coffee together with ethical reassurance explain about cultural capitalism?} : {The purchase includes a feeling of social responsibility, allowing consumption and moral compensation for consumption to occur in the same transaction.} [Source: RSA lecture, _First as Tragedy, Then as Farce_](https://www.thersa.org/videos/rsa-animate-first-as-tragedy-then-as-farce/)
    
29. {Why does Žižek invoke Oscar Wilde’s criticism of charity in that lecture?} : {Charity can alleviate suffering while helping preserve the property relations producing it, so relief must be distinguished from transforming the conditions that reproduce poverty.} [Source](https://www.thersa.org/videos/rsa-animate-first-as-tragedy-then-as-farce/)
    

### B. Production and exchange

30. {Why does Žižek criticize planned economies that produce goods people do not want?} : {Production cannot be understood apart from consumption and circulation, because producing an object does not guarantee that it meets needs or successfully enters social exchange.} [Source: _The Parallax View_, excerpt III](https://www.lacan.com/zizparallax3.htm)
    
31. {Why does Žižek reject an absolute opposition between productive capitalism and financial speculation?} : {Profit-driven expansion already organizes capitalist production, making speculative accumulation an expression of its underlying logic rather than merely an external corruption of an otherwise innocent economy.} [Source](https://www.lacan.com/zizparallax3.htm)
    

## Topic 8: Violence, power, and liberation

### A. Violence within ordinary life

32. {What does Žižek’s account of Nikolai Lossky’s comfortable life before his Soviet expulsion illustrate about systemic violence?} : {A sincerely benevolent person can overlook the exploitation sustaining ordinary comfort, because violence built into social arrangements remains less visible than the disruptive violence experienced personally.} [Source: _Violence_, publisher’s excerpt](https://us.macmillan.com/books/9780312427184/violence/)
    
33. {Why does Žižek distinguish visible violent outbreaks from the apparently peaceful background against which they occur?} : {Treating disruption as the sole location of violence conceals the coercion and deprivation reproduced by the normal functioning of institutions and economic relations.} [Source](https://us.macmillan.com/books/9780312427184/violence/)
    
34. {What does Žižek’s example of philanthropists whose speculation damages livelihoods reveal about opposing violence?} : {Opposition to visible suffering can coexist with participation in the economic mechanisms that generate it, so humanitarian concern does not by itself establish structural innocence.} [Source](https://us.macmillan.com/books/9780312427184/violence/)
    

### B. Liberation and its costs

35. {Why does Žižek use _Fight Club_ to argue that liberation hurts?} : {Domination can bind people through enjoyment as well as coercion, making liberation painful because it requires surrendering satisfactions that helped sustain their attachment to power.} [Source: “Liberation Hurts”](https://www.lacan.com/zizekillinois.htm)
    
36. {How does Žižek clarify his provocative discussion of family killing in _The Usual Suspects_ and _Beloved_?} : {He uses these extreme narratives to examine breaking power’s hold through cherished attachments and explicitly rejects the interpretation that ethical action requires literally killing one’s child.} [Source](https://www.lacan.com/zizekillinois.htm)
    
37. {Why does Žižek question tolerance that accepts others only while they remain distant?} : {Such tolerance can preserve separation while presenting itself as openness, because it welcomes difference only after excluding the disturbing proximity of another person’s actual life.} [Source](https://www.lacan.com/zizekillinois.htm)
    

## Topic 9: Revolution, communism, and universality

### A. Revolution requires reconstruction

38. {Why does Žižek distinguish Russia’s February Revolution from Lenin’s intervention leading toward October?} : {Removing the old ruler opens possibilities but leaves unresolved the harder task of transforming the institutions and assumptions through which social power operates.} [Source: “Revolution Must Strike Twice”](https://www.lrb.co.uk/the-paper/v24/n14/slavoj-zizek/revolution-must-strike-twice)
    
39. {What does Žižek’s discussion of Lenin confronting demands for guaranteed revolutionary success explain about political action?} : {Transformative action proceeds without a final historical guarantee, requiring responsibility for an intervention whose outcome cannot be secured beforehand by necessity or prediction.} [Source](https://www.lrb.co.uk/the-paper/v24/n14/slavoj-zizek/revolution-must-strike-twice)
    

### B. The commons and the excluded

40. {Why does Žižek discuss software monopolies, environmental resources, and genetic inheritance together?} : {They exemplify shared conditions of collective existence whose enclosure motivates his renewed communist concern with organizing access beyond private appropriation.} [Source: “Censorship Today”](https://www.lacan.com/zizecology1.htm)
    
41. {Why does Žižek place slum dwellers and other excluded populations at the center of emancipatory politics?} : {A politics claiming universality must confront those denied a recognized place within its social order, because protecting shared resources only for established insiders preserves exclusion.} [Source](https://www.lacan.com/zizecology1.htm)
    

## Topic 10: Ecology and contingency

### A. Nature provides no guaranteed balance

42. {What philosophical point does Žižek make when invoking prehistoric catastrophes and the origins of oil?} : {He uses them to challenge the image of nature as a permanently harmonious order, arguing that contingency and disruption belong to natural history itself.} [Source: “Censorship Today”](https://www.lacan.com/zizecology1.htm)
    
43. {Why does Žižek criticize environmental rhetoric that treats every radical intervention as a dangerous violation of nature?} : {Such rhetoric can convert ecological concern into conservative distrust of collective transformation, even when existing social arrangements themselves threaten the conditions of life.} [Source](https://www.lacan.com/zizecology1.htm)
    

## Topic 11: Christianity, atheism, and materialism

### A. Divine division

44. {Why does Žižek emphasize Christ’s experience of abandonment on the cross?} : {He reads it as division within God himself, so human alienation from divine assurance becomes part of the Christian divine experience rather than merely a failure of human understanding.} [Source: “Only a Suffering God Can Save Us”](https://www.lacan.com/zizshadowplay.html)
    
45. {Why does Žižek distinguish Christ’s incarnation from a morally exceptional person resembling God?} : {Incarnation locates the universal divine in a contingent human individual, making divine identity irreducible to a collection of superior personal qualities.} [Source](https://www.lacan.com/zizshadowplay.html)
    

### B. Criticism from within religion

46. {Why does Žižek discuss a story in which an atheist discovers that an afterlife exists but finds it unbearable?} : {Accepting a religious premise can expose its unsettling consequences from within, showing that an afterlife’s existence would not automatically establish its goodness or provide meaningful consolation.} [Source: “Is God Dead, Unconscious, Evil, Impotent, Stupid … Or Just Counterfactual?”](https://zizekstudies.org/index.php/IJZS/article/download/97/96)

## Topic 12: Cinema, art, and the gaze

### A. Seeing from outside oneself

47. {Why does Žižek interpret Vertov’s camera in _Kino-Eye_ as an autonomous eye?} : {The camera separates vision from an individual’s ordinary bodily perspective, enabling cinema to reorganize perception rather than merely reproduce familiar experience.} [Source: “Only a Suffering God Can Save Us”](https://www.lacan.com/zizshadowplay.html)
    
48. {What does Žižek’s account of unexpectedly seeing his own profile through mirrors reveal about self-perception?} : {An unfamiliar view of one’s own body exposes the gap between lived self-experience and externally visible appearance, disrupting the assumption that the self transparently coincides with its image.} [Source](https://www.lacan.com/zizshadowplay.html)
