## Topic 1: Chromatography Principles

### 1.1 Separation and chromatograms

2. {What information does a chromatogram provide?} : {A chromatogram plots detector response against time, showing peaks whose positions help identify components and whose areas or heights can be used to measure their amounts after calibration.}/{detector response}, {peaks}, {areas or heights}, {calibration}
    
3. {What is retention time, tᵣ?} : {Retention time is the time between sample injection and the appearance of an analyte’s peak maximum at the detector.}/{sample injection}, {peak maximum}, {detector}
    
4. {What is the mobile-phase hold-up time, tₘ?} : {The mobile-phase hold-up time is the time required for an [^6]unretained substance to travel through the chromatographic system.}/{hold-up time}, {unretained substance}, {chromatographic system}
    
5. {What is adjusted retention time, tᵣ′?} : {Adjusted retention time is the analyte’s retention time minus the mobile-phase hold-up time, expressed as tᵣ′ = tᵣ − tₘ.}/{adjusted retention time}, {hold-up time}, {tᵣ′ = tᵣ − tₘ}
    
6. {How is the retention factor, k, calculated?} : {The retention factor is calculated as k = (tᵣ − tₘ)/tₘ = tᵣ′/tₘ and describes analyte retention relative to an unretained substance.}/{retention factor}, {k = (tᵣ − tₘ)/tₘ = tᵣ′/tₘ}, {unretained substance}
    
7. {What is the distribution coefficient, K?} : {The distribution coefficient is K = Cₛ/Cₘ, where Cₛ and Cₘ are the equilibrium analyte concentrations in the stationary and mobile phases, respectively.}/{distribution coefficient}, {K = Cₛ/Cₘ}, {equilibrium analyte concentrations}
    
8. {How are the distribution coefficient and retention factor related?} : {The retention factor is k = K(Vₛ/Vₘ), so retention depends on both the analyte’s distribution coefficient and the relative volumes of the stationary and mobile phases.}/{k = K(Vₛ/Vₘ)}, {distribution coefficient}, {relative volumes}
    
9. {What is chromatographic selectivity, α?} : {Selectivity is α = k₂/k₁ for two analytes with k₂ > k₁ and describes how differently the chromatographic system retains them.}/{selectivity}, {α = k₂/k₁}, {k₂ > k₁}
    
10. {What are retention volume and retention volume factor?} : {Retention volume is the mobile-phase volume required to [^7]elute an analyte, and the corresponding volume-based retention factor is calculated analogously to the time-based retention factor.}/{retention volume}, {mobile-phase volume}, {volume-based retention factor}

### 1.3 Column efficiency and peak broadening
