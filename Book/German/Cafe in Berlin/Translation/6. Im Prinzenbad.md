It’s the end of August. It rains a lot, but it’s not that cold yet. Today the sun is shining a little. Ted had the idea of going swimming. But where?

“Wannsee beach,” said Ted.

“How do we get there?” I asked.

Ted tapped something on his phone and said, “First we take the M29 bus, then the S1 train.”

“What about the Badeschiff?” I asked.

“Is that an indoor pool?” Chang asked.

“No,” I said. “It’s a floating pool in the River Spree.”

“A floating swimming pool in the river?” Chang asked. “I don’t understand.”

“You can’t swim directly in the Spree. The river is too dirty,” I said.

“And the Badeschiff is infested with hipsters,” said Ted.

Chang said, “Or we could go to the Baltic Sea.”

Ted laughed. “Are you crazy?” he asked.

“Why?” I asked.

Ted tapped on his phone and said, “It’s more than three hours by train from here to the Baltic Sea.”

“Oh, okay,” said Chang.

In the end, Ted, Chang, and I went to the Prinzenbad. It’s an outdoor swimming pool just a few minutes away.

At the entrance stood a man wearing a T-shirt that said “Security.” He searched our bags.

“What are you looking for?” Chang asked.

“Knives, batons, guns?” he said.

We shook our heads. Then we bought admission tickets. Four euros per person.

The Prinzenbad is an interesting place. You hear a lot of Arabic, Turkish, and German here. You see girls in bikinis next to women in “burkinis.” Groups of Turkish teenagers play in the baby pool. German mothers scold their children. Security guards patrol. Babies scream. It smells of chlorine and fries.

The water was a good temperature. Not too warm, not too cold. Swimming was difficult. The children kept jumping into the water from the side. The lifeguard called through his megaphone, “No jumping from the edge of the pool!” But the children didn’t care.

After swimming, we ate fries. Red and white—with mayonnaise and ketchup. Then we were tired.

“It’s nice here,” said Chang.

“Wannsee is better,” said Ted.

“Next time,” I said.

Then we heard thunder. The sky turned black. It began to rain.
