## 1

I just want to go on a day trip. A really nice day trip.

Leave very early on Saturday morning and come back very late on Saturday evening.

Even if I have to go alone. Why not?

By train or by bus. Maybe to the Costa Brava.

But not just to go swimming. It's only the beginning of June.

I could also do some sightseeing, go for a walk, and perhaps have a picnic.

The main thing is to get out of the city.

## 2

On Thursday evening, I tell my friends Carmen and Pablo about my plan. They've invited me over for dinner.

The two of them listen with interest, and then Pablo asks:

"But why don't we go together?"

He seems almost a little offended that I hadn't suggested it myself.

But I didn't know they had time. Or that they enjoyed this kind of trip.

Of course, it's a good idea. A trip is much nicer with friends.

Besides, Pablo wants to go by car. That's more comfortable, and we can also visit a few beautiful villages along the way.

I'm delighted.

They don't have a car, but they have friends who do.

"No problem," Pablo says. He'll organize it.

"So, very early on Saturday morning," I say.

"Sure," Carmen replies. "Eleven o'clock. Agreed?"

Well, eleven o'clock isn't early for me. It's actually quite late.

Eleven o'clock is late morning, not early morning.

But in Spanish, there isn't really a distinction between the two. There's just mañana.

Besides, both of them have to get up early all week.

"All right, eleven o'clock."

## 3

Saturday morning.

I got up at half past nine, went shopping, and made a few sandwiches for the picnic.

Carmen and Pablo are supposed to take care of the drinks.

I ring their doorbell.

It's ten past eleven.

Nothing happens.

Perhaps they're just picking up the car, I think.

But suddenly I hear footsteps, and the door opens.

There's Pablo, wearing his pajamas.

He smiles warmly and tells me to come in.

A little confused, I ask whether I've woken him up.

"No, no," he says, waving the question away. He's already been awake.

"And Carmen?"

"In the bathroom," Pablo yawns, shuffling into the kitchen.

"Coffee?" he asks.

I hear the shower being turned on in the bathroom.

"Yes," I say. "Why not?"

"Have a seat," says Pablo, rubbing his eyes and putting the coffee pot on the stove.

I ask whether everything worked out with the car.

Pablo looks at me questioningly.

"Oh, right," he finally says. "Good thing you reminded me."

He goes into the living room.

I hear him talking on the phone.

Then he comes back, scratches his head, and pours the coffee.

"Is something wrong?" I ask.

"No, no, everything's fine, it's just..."

He pauses and scratches his head again.

"It's just that Clara and Salvador can't lend us their car. They're going to Tarragona to visit his parents."

I look at my watch.

It's twenty-five to twelve.

There's a bus to Tossa at twelve. Better than nothing.

Pablo takes a sip of coffee and rubs his nose.

"Wait, we have other friends."

He goes back into the living room.

Once again, I hear him talking.

This time, he returns smiling.

"There you go," he says, patting me on the shoulder. "You just need a little patience. Everything's sorted. We're getting a car. From David and Gemma. They don't need it. They're going shopping here in the city center."

He looks out of the window.

"Unbelievable, in weather like this."

Just then, Carmen comes into the kitchen, greets me warmly, and wraps a towel around her hair.

"So, can we leave now?" I ask.

It's five to twelve.

"Of course," says Pablo. "Are you ready, darling?"

"Sure, sweetheart," says Carmen, smiling as she pours herself a cup of coffee.

## 4

Exactly an hour and a half later, we get into the car.

We didn't have any wine yet, so we bought two bottles at the market.

We also bought olive oil, salt, and washing powder — not for the picnic, but for tomorrow.

After all, you can't go shopping on Sundays.

Then we went to David and Gemma's place to pick up the car keys.

They live only ten minutes away.

They had just made coffee, so naturally we had another cup with them.

They wanted to know what we were planning to do.

So Carmen told them about the Costa Brava, our picnic, the walk, and the beautiful villages.

Gemma and David thought the plan sounded really good.

They would have liked to go on a trip themselves.

But they urgently needed to go shopping.

They needed a new sofa for their living room.

After half an hour, we said goodbye.

But we still couldn't leave.

We first had to find the car.

They hadn't used it for two weeks, so they couldn't remember exactly where they'd parked it.

## 5

So, shortly before half past one, we're finally sitting in the car.

But we're still not leaving.

Pablo has had an idea.

There's room for five people in the car, so we could take someone else along.

Daniela and Luis, for example.

"Call them and ask," I suggest.

But Pablo doesn't have their telephone number with him.

"No problem," says Pablo.

Daniela and Luis live in Gràcia, which is practically on our way.

We'll just stop by for a moment.

## 6

At quarter past two, we finally set off.

Daniela and Luis were actually at home, but they couldn't come with us because Daniela's parents were coming over for lunch.

So the three of us go alone.

We stop just one more time.

Carmen wants to buy a newspaper.

On Saturdays, it always contains the television schedule for the entire week.

We're making very slow progress.

There's a lot of traffic.

Of course, lots of people are driving out to the countryside for lunch.

It takes us almost an hour to drive thirty kilometers.

At some point, I'm no longer sure: are these people really still driving out to the countryside for lunch, or are they already coming back from shopping in the city?

Eventually, Pablo asks:

"Tell me, do we really have to go all the way to the Costa Brava?"

"No," Carmen replies. "I don't particularly need to."

Neither does Pablo.

He knows a beautiful beach very close by.

It's just off the next exit.

Besides, Pablo and Carmen are already getting a little hungry.

"Why should we sit in the car for so much longer? We can always drive through the villages later," Pablo decides.

I agree.

We turn off the road and drive to the beautiful beach.

## 7

I know this beach.

"Well, isn't this beach wonderful? I've been wanting to show it to you for ages."

"Yes," I say. "The beach really is very beautiful."

I don't mention that I already know it.

It's right next to the railway line. You can get here by train. Just forty minutes from Barcelona.

Pablo also knows a good restaurant.

Good and inexpensive.

"Would you like some paella?" Pablo asks.

Carmen would like some, and I enjoy paella too.

"All right," Pablo says. "Then we'll have a nice meal now, and afterward we'll go to the beach."

"Okay," I say, leaving the bag with the sandwiches in the car.

## 8

Five o'clock.

"Man, I'm tired," says Carmen. "Time for a siesta."

The paella took a long time.

First, we waited twenty minutes for a table, and then another twenty minutes for the paella.

Fortunately, the white wine arrived immediately.

Very good and very cold.

We drank almost two bottles.

We lie down on the beach.

Pablo falls asleep immediately.

Carmen reads the television schedule for a while and then falls asleep too.

I look out at the sea, then go for a swim and walk along the beach for a little while.

Really beautiful.

## 9

Quarter to six.

Carmen and Pablo are awake now.

She's reading the newspaper, and he's standing in the water up to his knees.

"Listen," Carmen says to me, "I've just been talking to Pablo..."

She pauses.

I drop down onto the sand.

"You see," she continues, "since we have the car today, we thought..."

She pauses again.

"...that maybe we could stop by the furniture store later. I mean, it's right on the way, and we urgently need some deck chairs. For the terrace. And flowerpots. And I'm sure you need something too."

She squints into the evening sun and looks at me questioningly.

"Well," I say, "I don't actually need anything. But that's all right. I'll just come along anyway."

"Really? Fantastic!" Carmen says, calling Pablo.

Pablo slowly comes out of the water.

Carmen looks at her watch.

"The thing is, it's almost six already, and the store closes at eight. So we should leave soon. Right now would be best. Is that okay?"

"No problem," says Pablo, shaking the sand off his stomach.

"Let's go."

## 10

We don't arrive at the furniture store until half past seven because of the heavy traffic.

All the day-trippers are returning from the Costa Brava.

But that's not a problem. The furniture store is open until nine.

Carmen and Pablo still have enough time to choose their deck chairs and flowerpots.

At the checkout, we run into Gemma and David.

They couldn't find a sofa anywhere in the city, so they also came out to this furniture store.

By subway.

No problem.

They've already discovered something and want to show it to us immediately.

So we go back to the living room department and look at a sofa.

It's green, but they've ordered it in yellow, Gemma explains. That's why it will take another two weeks to arrive.

Carmen and Pablo think the sofa is fantastic and very practical.

And outside, by the car, Gemma and David think Carmen and Pablo's deck chairs and flowerpots are also fantastic and very practical.

"So, what did you buy?" Gemma asks me.

"Nothing," I say.

"What?" Gemma asks in astonishment. "You didn't find anything at all?"

"No," I reply. "I wasn't looking for anything."

"Oh, I see," Gemma says, turning back to Carmen.

## 11

Finally, we all drive back to the city together.

It's a bit cramped because of the flowerpots, but we manage.

"Oh, by the way," David asks at some point, "how was your trip?"

"Lovely," Pablo replies. "Really fantastic. We had wonderful paella, and the water was beautiful, pleasantly cool."

"And much cleaner than here in the city," Carmen adds.

Then we say goodbye.

David asks whether we'd like to come over to their place to watch a film.

They could also show us where the new sofa is going to go.

But Carmen and Pablo want to put the flowerpots and deck chairs on their terrace right away.

I quickly help them.

They invite me to stay for dinner.

But I'd rather go home.

Take a shower and so on.

"As you wish," says Pablo, lying down in one of the deck chairs.

"But come over for lunch tomorrow."

## 12

It's eleven o'clock at night.

I got home a few minutes ago.

I've just realized there's nothing to eat in the kitchen.

Absolutely nothing.

So I was already thinking I'd have to go out again.

Even though I'm so tired.

But now I've remembered that I still have the picnic food.

The cheese sandwiches are a little soggy, and the wine is much too warm.

But it's better than nothing.

I'll eat one or two sandwiches and put the rest in the refrigerator.

Then I'll take a shower and go to bed soon.

Tomorrow at nine o'clock, there's a bus to Tossa.

End of "Der Ausflug" (The Day Trip).

## Understanding the ending

The humor of the story comes from the contrast between the narrator's original plan and what actually happens.

The narrator wants to spend an entire Saturday exploring the Costa Brava, visiting beautiful villages, walking, and enjoying a picnic. But Carmen and Pablo are so relaxed and disorganized that the day becomes a series of delays, detours, meals, and shopping trips.

The biggest irony is that they spend hours organizing a car and driving, only to arrive at a beach the narrator could have reached by train from Barcelona in forty minutes.

The final sentence is especially important:

> Tomorrow at nine o'clock, there's a bus to Tossa.

The narrator has decided to make the trip after all, probably alone this time.

For your B1 German studies, pay particular attention to the expressions kein Problem (no problem), einverstanden sein (to agree), vorbeifahren (to stop by or drive past), sich etwas anschauen (to take a look at something), and losfahren (to set off).

The story is also useful for recognizing how German alternates between the Präsens and Perfekt to tell a lively, conversational story.
