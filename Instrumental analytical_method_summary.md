1. Chromatography

| Method                                            | Core idea                                                                                                                           |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Thin-layer chromatography (TLC)**               | Substances travel {different distances} across a plate because they interact differently with the {stationary layer} and {solvent}. |
| **Gas chromatography (GC)**                       | {Vaporised substances} separate according to their {volatility} and interactions with the column’s stationary phase.                |
| **High-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC)** | A {pressurised liquid} carries substances through a column, where different interactions cause {different retention times.}         |
| **Normal-phase chromatography**                   | A {polar stationary phase} generally retains more {polar substances} more strongly.                                                 |
| **Reversed-phase chromatography**                 | A {nonpolar stationary phase} generally retains more {hydrophobic substances} more strongly.                                        |
| **HILIC**                                         | A {polar stationary phase} with an {organic-rich mobile phase} retains and separates {polar substances.}                            |
| **Size-exclusion chromatography (SEC)**           | Separation depends on {access to pores}: large molecules generally elute first because they enter fewer pores.                      |
| **Ion-exchange chromatography**                   | {Charged substances} bind {reversibly} to {oppositely charged} groups on the stationary phase.                                      |

2. Spectroscopy

| Method                                             | Core idea                                                                                                                                  |
| -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Infrared spectroscopy (IR)**                     | Molecules absorb {infrared light} when {vibrations} change their {dipole moment}, revealing {bonds} and {functional groups}.               |
| **Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR)** | An {interferometer} records a {combined} infrared signal, which a Fourier transform converts into an {absorption spectrum}.                |
| **Attenuated total reflection (ATR)**              | An {evanescent field} from an {infrared-transparent crystal} probes a sample {touching its surface.}                                       |
| **Near-infrared spectroscopy (NIR)**               | {Overtone} and {combination-band absorption} patterns support identification and concentration measurement through {calibration}.          |
| **Raman spectroscopy**                             | {Light} exchanges {energy} with {molecular vibrations} that change {polarisability}, producing {frequency-shifted scattered} light.        |
| **Mass spectrometry (MS)**                         | Substances become {gas-phase ions} that are measured according to their {mass-to-charge} ratio, revealing mass and structural information. |
| **ICP–MS**                                         | A {hot argon plasma} produces elemental ions, which a mass spectrometer measures for elemental analysis.                                   |
| **ICP–OES**                                        | A {hot argon plasma} {excites} atoms and ions, whose characteristic {emitted light} identifies and quantifies elements.                    |
| **Laser-ablation ICP analysis**                    | A laser {removes material} from {selected locations} so its elemental composition can be mapped across a surface or through depth.         |
3. Electrophoresis

| Method                                            | Core idea                                                                                                                                |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Capillary zone electrophoresis (CZE)**          | {Charged} substances move through a {capillary} at different rates according to their charge, size, and the liquid’s viscosity.          |
| **Capillary gel electrophoresis**                 | A {polymer-filled} capillary acts as a {molecular sieve}, separating substances mainly by {size}.                                        |
| **Gel electrophoresis**                           | An electric field moves substances through a {gel}, which restricts {larger} molecules more strongly.                                    |
| **SDS–PAGE**                                      | SDS gives proteins {similar charge} per unit mass, allowing a {polyacrylamide} gel to separate them mainly by {molecular mass}.          |
| **Micellar electrokinetic chromatography (MEKC)** | Substances partition differently between {moving micelles} and the {surrounding liquid}, allowing even {neutral substances} to separate. |
| **Isoelectric focusing (IEF)**                    | Proteins move through a {pH gradient} until reaching their {isoelectric point}, where their {net charge is zero}.                        |
| **Two-dimensional protein electrophoresis**       | {Proteins} are separated first by {isoelectric point} and then by {molecular mass}.                                                      |
| **Electroblotting**                               | An electric field transfers {separated substances} from a {gel} to a {membrane} for detection with antibodies or probes.                 |
4. Mass spectroscopy

| Method                                              | Core idea                                                                                                                 |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Electron ionisation (EI)**                        | Energetic electrons {remove electrons} from molecules, often producing extensive fragmentation useful for identification. |
| **Chemical ionisation (CI)**                        | {Reagent-gas ions} react with analytes, usually causing less fragmentation than EI.                                       |
| **Electrospray ionisation (ESI)**                   | High voltage creates {charged solution droplets} that lose solvent and release ions, often with multiple charges.         |
| **Atmospheric-pressure chemical ionisation (APCI)** | A {corona discharge} creates {reagent ions} that {ionise vaporised analytes} through chemical reactions.                  |
| **MALDI**                                           | A {laser} and an {absorbing matrix} {release and ionise analytes} with relatively little fragmentation.                   |
